the approach

Identifying Models

  • Search on OpenAlex (OA) for model, equation, process, map, automaton.
  • Retrieve 1M random records.
  • Use gpt-6.1 Sol to filter raw strings: “SIR model” vs. “mouse model”
  • Arrange by shortest path on substring similarity, merge in batches & filter.
  • 3,380 candidate models. Search canonical names on OA.
  • Keep 1,338 models with more than 1,000 mentions.

Researching Models

  • gpt-6.1 Sol with web access on high reasoning refines OA queries and checks them against common problems.
    • “SIR model” or “Susceptible–Infected–Recovered model” or “…”
  • Download 5,000 random records per model, total 5.63M abstracts.
  • Embed and project onto a basemap (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)

Part V
The structure of
model transfer

k-nearest neighbors Citations between the papers that use kNN graphs.

k-nearest neighbors Main path analysis: the paths most travelled through the citation graph (SPLC).
Hummon and Doreian (1989) Batagelj (2003)

k-nearest neighbors SPLC aggregated by genieclust clusters: flow between regions of the map.

Spread How many map regions a model covers (x), and how far apart they are (y). Many models sit high on both.

  • x: effective number of regions (exp entropy), median 5.6. y: mean distance between those regions, 1 = as far apart as two random regions, median 0.76.
  • Broad spread mostly comes with distance: the more regions, the farther apart they tend to be.
  • Ising, Lotka–Volterra, Hodgkin–Huxley, Kuramoto and Schelling all sit above both medians.
  • Top left: few regions, but far apart (Two-Higgs-Doublet, GARCH, Doyle–Fuller–Newman).
  • Bottom: several regions close together (Friedewald, Ramberg–Osgood, Intelligent Driver Model).
  • Top right: generic statistical machinery (logistic regression, cure and accelerated failure time models).

Homes Distribution of models across main nodes (mean share 52%).

  • Home = the field where most of a model’s papers sit. Open circle: expected count if models were homed in proportion to the field’s share of all papers.
  • Mathematics (×6.5), physics (×4.1), computer science (×3.0) and economics (×2.7) produce far more models than their size predicts.
  • Engineering is the largest home in absolute terms, but only ×1.5.
  • Medicine, the life sciences, psychology and the social sciences use many models but are home to few (×0.1–0.4).
  • So models tend to flow out of the formal and physical sciences into the rest.

Symmetry How one-way a model’s flow between regions is (x), and whether its home region exports or imports it (y).

  • x: how much more one-way the flow between regions is than random direction would give (observed − chance). 0 = as reciprocal as random, median 0.21.
  • y: is the home region a net exporter (+1) or importer (−1) of the model. Median +0.16, so homes mostly export.
  • The two go together: the more one-way the flow, the more extreme the home’s role.
  • Upper right, home exports in one direction: Snowmelt Runoff, Stoner, Kohn–Sham, Variable Infiltration Capacity.
  • Lower right, home imports in one direction: GARCH, Beta Regression, Fine–Gray, Panel Threshold.
  • Far left, near-reciprocal exchange: Altman Z-score, Korteweg–de Vries, Lyapunov, Kuramoto; Lotka–Volterra, Schelling, Hodgkin–Huxley close by.
  • Point size: OpenAlex works naming the model.

Summary of first results

  • Many models are local only, but a decent share travels widely.
  • There is no single centre of model origin: models arise in several centres, but not uniformly.
  • Flow between areas tends to be asymmetrical.

Some qualifications & further work

  • OpenAlex is not perfect.
  • We’re limited to sources that include abstracts (recency bias) and citations.
  • Undersampling of citations.
  • Expert review.
  • Various UMAP issues.
